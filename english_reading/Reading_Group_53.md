## Article 157: The Science of Orthopedics

Orthopedics addresses musculoskeletal conditions including bones, joints, ligaments, tendons, and muscles. The skeleton provides structure, protects organs, and enables movement through muscle-powered leverage. Orthopedic surgeons treat trauma, degeneration, and congenital conditions.

Fractures require alignment and immobilization for healing. Casting and splinting stabilize breaks; surgical fixation with plates, screws, and rods provides rigid internal fixation. Healing time varies with bone type and patient age.

Joint disorders include osteoarthritis from cartilage breakdown and rheumatoid arthritis from autoimmune inflammation. Conservative treatment involves physical therapy and medication. Joint replacement surgery, especially hip and knee, dramatically improves function in end-stage disease.

Sports medicine manages athletic injuries including ligament tears, muscle strains, and stress fractures. Arthroscopic techniques enable minimally invasive joint evaluation and treatment. Spine care addresses disc herniation, spinal stenosis, and deformity.

## Article 158: The History of Literature

Literature, written compositions, spans from ancient epics to contemporary experiments. Gilgamesh, the oldest known story, reflects Mesopotamian concerns. Greek tragedies explored fate, hubris, and divine authority. Sanskrit epics including Ramayana shaped Indian civilization. Chinese classical literature established aesthetic and moral standards.

Medieval literature featured romances, allegories, and religious texts. Dante's Divine Comedy synthesized medieval knowledge. Chaucer vernacularized English literature with The Canterbury Tales. Renaissance humanism revived classical models while celebrating individual potential.

Shakespeare's plays captured human nature with unparalleled depth. The novel emerged as dominant form, with Cervantes, Austen, and Dickens pioneering the genre. Modernism fragmented narrative conventions; postmodernism questioned representation itself. Contemporary literature embraces global perspectives and digital formats.

Poetry, the most concentrated literary form, persists through millennia. Epic, lyric, and dramatic modes evolved through countless cultural contexts. Literary criticism interprets texts, revealing hidden meanings and historical contexts. Literature remains essential for understanding human experience.

## Article 159: The Science of Ophthalmology

Ophthalmology studies the eye and visual system. Light entering the eye passes through cornea, pupil, and lens, focusing on the retina. Photoreceptor cells in the retina, rods for dim light and cones for color, convert light to neural signals transmitted via the optic nerve.

Refractive errors prevent proper focus. Myopia, nearsightedness, occurs when the eyeball is too long; hyperopia, farsightedness, when too short. Astigmatism involves irregular corneal curvature. Glasses, contacts, and refractive surgery correct these errors.

Cataracts cloud the lens, typically with aging. Surgical removal with intraocular lens implantation restores vision. Glaucoma involves optic nerve damage from elevated pressure, threatening peripheral vision first. Treatment includes drops, lasers, and surgery to lower intraocular pressure.

Age-related macular degeneration affects central vision; treatment includes injections slowing progression. Diabetic retinopathy damages retinal vessels; laser and anti-VEGF injections preserve sight.
