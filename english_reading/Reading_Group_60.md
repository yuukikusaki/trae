## Article 178: The Science of Psychology

Psychology, the scientific study of behavior and mental processes, encompasses cognition, emotion, perception, motivation, and social interaction. Research methods include experiments, observations, and case studies.

Clinical psychology diagnoses and treats mental disorders. Depression involves persistent sadness and loss of interest. Anxiety disorders feature excessive worry and fear. Psychotherapy and medication address these conditions.

Developmental psychology examines changes throughout the lifespan. Infancy involves rapid cognitive and motor development. Adolescence brings physical maturation and identity formation. Aging presents cognitive challenges and opportunities for wisdom.

Social psychology investigates how others influence individuals. Conformity, obedience, and group polarization affect decisions. Cognitive psychology explores attention, memory, and problem-solving. Neuroscience methods reveal brain mechanisms underlying behavior.

## Article 179: The Geography of Tundra

Tundra, treeless regions in Arctic, Antarctic, and alpine zones, feature harsh conditions limiting plant growth. Short growing seasons, extreme cold, and permafrost prevent tree establishment. Vegetation consists of grasses, sedges, mosses, and lichens.

Arctic tundra extends across northern latitudes. Antarctic tundra occurs on sub-Antarctic islands. Alpine tundra exists above treeline on mountains worldwide.

Extremely cold temperatures characterize tundra climates. Permafrost underlies most tundra, preventing deep root penetration. Summer thaw creates waterlogged surface soils.

Tundra ecosystems support specialized wildlife. Caribou migrate across Arctic tundra. Musk oxen and arctic foxes exemplify cold adaptations. Climate change disproportionately affects tundra regions.

## Article 180: The Science of Ecology

Ecology studies interactions between organisms and their environments. Ecosystems comprise living communities and physical surroundings. Energy flows through food webs; nutrients cycle through biogeochemical pathways.

Population ecology examines factors affecting species abundances. Predator-prey cycles, competition, and cooperation shape populations. Community ecology investigates how species interact within assemblages. Ecosystem ecology integrates these levels.

Biomes, major regional ecosystem types, include forests, grasslands, deserts, and tundra. Climate, particularly temperature and precipitation, determines biome distribution. Disturbance regimes including fire and flooding shape ecosystem dynamics.

Human activities have transformed ecosystems globally. Habitat destruction fragments populations. Pollution introduces toxins into food webs. Climate change shifts species distributions and disrupts interactions. Conservation ecology aims to preserve biodiversity and ecosystem services.
