## Article 124: The Science of Pharmacology

Pharmacology studies drugs, their mechanisms of action, therapeutic uses, and adverse effects. Drug development follows a long, expensive path from discovery to clinical use. Rigorous testing ensures safety and efficacy before regulatory approval.

Pharmacokinetics describes how bodies absorb, distribute, metabolize, and excrete drugs. Absorption varies with formulation and route. Distribution depends on protein binding and tissue penetration. Liver enzymes metabolize many drugs; kidneys excrete metabolites.

Pharmacodynamics describes drug effects on the body. Agonists activate receptors; antagonists block activation. Efficacy measures maximum effect; potency reflects dose required. Therapeutic index indicates margin between effective and toxic doses.

Adverse drug reactions range from mild to fatal. Allergic reactions involve immune mechanisms. Idiosyncratic reactions reflect individual variations. Drug interactions occur when substances alter absorption, metabolism, or effect.

## Article 125: The History of Law

Law, the system of rules governing conduct, has evolved from customary practices to sophisticated legal codes. Early law emerged from religious prescriptions and community norms. The Code of Hammurabi, promulgated around 1754 BCE, represented early codification. Roman law influenced Western legal traditions profoundly.

Common law, developed in England, relies on judicial precedents rather than comprehensive codes. Civil law systems, derived from Roman law, organize principles into systematic codes. Both traditions recognize fundamental rights and procedural fairness.

Constitutional law establishes government structures and limits. Criminal law defines offenses against society and prescribes punishments. Civil law addresses private disputes over contracts, property, and torts. Administrative law governs agency actions.

International law addresses relations between sovereign states. Treaties establish binding obligations. Customary international law emerges from consistent state practice accompanied by opinio juris. International courts adjudicate disputes and punish violations.

## Article 126: The Science of Immunology

Immunology investigates the immune system, the body's defense against pathogens and abnormal cells. Innate immunity provides immediate, non-specific protection. Physical barriers, phagocytic cells, and antimicrobial proteins prevent infection.

Adaptive immunity develops more slowly but generates specific, lasting protection. B cells produce antibodies matching antigens. Antibody functions include neutralization, opsonization, and complement activation. T cells coordinate responses or kill infected cells directly.

Immunological memory enables rapid secondary responses. Memory B and T cells persist after infection or vaccination. Booster shots strengthen waning immunity. Herd immunity protects vulnerable individuals when coverage is high.

Immunodeficiency leaves patients vulnerable to opportunistic infections. HIV specifically targets helper T cells, progressively destroying immune function. Immunotherapy harnesses immune mechanisms to treat cancer. Checkpoint inhibitors release brakes on immune attacks against tumors.
