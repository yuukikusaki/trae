## Article 142: The Science of Rheumatology

Rheumatology addresses disorders affecting joints, muscles, bones, and connective tissues. These conditions cause pain, stiffness, swelling, and reduced mobility, affecting millions worldwide. Autoimmune inflammation underlies many rheumatic diseases.

Rheumatoid arthritis involves immune attack on synovial membranes lining joints. Symmetric swelling of small joints, especially in hands and feet, characterizes this condition. Untreated disease erodes bone and deforms joints. Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs slow progression.

Osteoarthritis represents cartilage breakdown with aging or injury. Weight-bearing joints including knees, hips, and spine bear the brunt. Pain worsens with use; stiffness improves with movement. Management includes exercise, pain relievers, and joint replacement.

Systemic lupus erythematosus affects multiple organ systems including skin, kidneys, and nervous system. Butterfly facial rash and photosensitivity are characteristic. Gout results from urate crystal deposition, causing intensely painful joint inflammation. Ankylosing spondylitis fuses the spine, limiting mobility.

## Article 143: The Geography of Mountains

Mountains, prominent elevated landforms, cover approximately 25 percent of Earth's land surface. They form through tectonic collision, volcanic activity, and erosion. The Himalayas rose from Indian-Eurasian plate collision; the Andes resulted from Pacific subduction.

Mountain environments feature dramatic elevation gradients. Temperature drops roughly six degrees Celsius per 1000 meters altitude gain. Vegetation changes from forests through alpine meadows to barren peaks. Endemic species evolve in isolated mountain refugia.

Mountain peoples have developed cultures adapted to challenging terrain. Sherpas assist Himalayan climbers. Andean farmers cultivate terraced fields. Tibetan nomads herd yak across high plateaus. Traditional practices preserve biodiversity and ecological knowledge.

Mountains provide essential ecosystem services. Rivers originating in mountains supply freshwater to billions downstream. Snowmelt provides dry-season water for agriculture. Mountains capture carbon and host unique biodiversity. Climate change threatens glaciers and water supplies.

## Article 144: The Science of Genetics

Genetics studies heredity, the transmission of traits from parents to offspring. Mendelian principles describe inheritance patterns for single-gene traits. Alleles, alternative gene versions, segregate during gamete formation and combine during fertilization.

DNA, deoxyribonucleic acid, encodes genetic information through nucleotide sequences. The double helix structure permits faithful replication. Genes, functional units, specify proteins through transcription and translation. Gene expression is regulated through promoters, enhancers, and epigenetic modifications.

Human genetics encompasses approximately 20,000 protein-coding genes distributed across 23 chromosome pairs. Variations range from single nucleotide changes to large deletions. Dominant alleles manifest in heterozygotes; recessive alleles require homozygosity.

Genetic technologies enable diagnosis, treatment, and modification. PCR amplifies tiny DNA quantities. DNA sequencing determines nucleotide order. CRISPR editing enables precise genome modification. Gene therapy treats inherited disorders by delivering functional genes.
