## Article 172: The Science of Virology

Virology studies viruses, infectious agents that blur the living-nonliving boundary. Virions contain genetic material within protein shells; some have lipid envelopes. Sizes range from 30 nanometers to over one micrometer.

Viral replication involves attachment, penetration, uncoating, genome replication, protein synthesis, assembly, and release. Bacteriophages infect bacteria, shaping microbial populations. Animal viruses cause diseases from mild respiratory infections to lethal hemorrhagic fevers.

Emerging viruses pose pandemic threats. Zoonotic transmission introduces novel pathogens into human populations. Deforestation and global travel accelerate spillover events. COVID-19 demonstrated how quickly novel viruses disrupt global health.

Antiviral therapies target specific viral mechanisms. Combination approaches reduce resistance emergence. Vaccine platforms including mRNA enable rapid development. Surveillance and rapid response capabilities remain essential.

## Article 173: The History of Journalism

Journalism, the collection and dissemination of news, has evolved from town criers to global digital networks. Early newspapers appeared in seventeenth-century Europe. The printing press enabled broader distribution; penny papers attracted working-class readers.

Wire services aggregated news from worldwide correspondents. Radio brought news into homes. Television added moving images, defining broadcast journalism.

Digital transformation disrupted traditional models. Online platforms enable citizen journalism alongside professional reporters. Social media accelerates dissemination while spreading misinformation. Revenue models shifted to sponsored content and paywalls.

Journalism ethics emphasize accuracy, fairness, and independence. Investigative journalism exposes corruption and holds power accountable. Press freedom remains threatened in many nations.

## Article 174: The Geography of Forests

Forests cover approximately thirty percent of Earth's land area, hosting most terrestrial biodiversity. Tropical rainforests feature highest species richness. Canopy layers create distinct habitats from ground-dwelling mammals to canopy specialists.

Temperate forests experience seasonal variation. Deciduous trees shed leaves in autumn. Coniferous taiga stretches across northern latitudes. Mediterranean forests feature drought-tolerant evergreens.

Forest ecology involves complex interactions. Mycorrhizal fungi extend root systems, exchanging nutrients. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria enrich soils. Predator-prey dynamics regulate populations.

Deforestation releases stored carbon and destroys habitat. Agricultural expansion, logging, and mining drive forest loss. Conservation strategies include protected areas and sustainable forestry.
