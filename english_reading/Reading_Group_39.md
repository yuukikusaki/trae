## Article 115: The Geography of Grasslands

Grasslands, dominated by grasses rather than trees or shrubs, cover approximately twenty percent of Earth's land surface. They occur in regions receiving insufficient rainfall for forests but enough for grasses. Prairies stretch across central North America; savannas combine grasses with scattered trees in Africa, South America, and Australia. Steppes extend across Eurasia.

Grassland ecosystems feature extreme temperatures, seasonal precipitation patterns, and periodic fires. Grasses survive grazing and fire through underground meristems that regenerate after damage. Root systems extend deep, accessing water and stabilizing soils. Drought-deciduous leaves conserve moisture.

Bison, kangaroos, and other grazers shape grassland dynamics through their foraging and nutrient cycling. Predators including wolves and lions regulate grazer populations. Prairie dog towns create habitat heterogeneity and shelter for other species.

Human conversion of grasslands for agriculture ranks among the most extensive land transformations. Crops including wheat, corn, and rice grow predominantly on former grasslands. Conservation occurs through protected areas and sustainable ranching practices that maintain ecological functions.

## Article 116: The Science of Hematology

Hematology studies blood, its disorders, and the hematopoietic system. Blood comprises plasma, red cells, white cells, and platelets. Red blood cells, lacking nuclei, transport oxygen via hemoglobin. White blood cells defend against infection. Platelets enable clotting.

Anemia results from insufficient red cells or hemoglobin. Iron deficiency anemia, the most common type, responds to dietary iron supplementation. Sickle cell disease involves abnormal hemoglobin causing rigid, crescent-shaped cells. Hemophilia impairs clotting through factor deficiencies.

White blood cell disorders include leukemias, cancers of blood-forming tissues. Acute leukemias progress rapidly; chronic forms develop gradually. Diagnosis involves blood counts, bone marrow examination, and genetic testing.

Transfusion medicine provides compatible blood products. Blood typing determines ABO and Rh compatibility. Apheresis separates blood components for targeted therapy. Stem cell transplantation can cure certain hematologic malignancies. Anticoagulants prevent or treat clots; thrombolytics dissolve existing clots.

## Article 117: The History of Journalism

Journalism, the collection and dissemination of news, has evolved from town criers to global digital networks. Early newspapers appeared in seventeenth-century Europe, read by literate elites. The printing press enabled broader distribution; penny papers in the nineteenth century attracted working-class readers.

Wire services including Associated Press and Reuters aggregated news from correspondents worldwide. Radio brought news into homes during the twentieth century. Television added moving images, defining broadcast journalism. Editorial independence distinguished professional journalism from propaganda.

Digital transformation disrupted traditional models. Online platforms enable citizen journalism alongside professional reporters. Social media accelerates news dissemination while spreading misinformation. Revenue models shifted from subscriptions and advertising to sponsored content and paywalls.

Journalism ethics emphasize accuracy, fairness, and independence. Verification, attribution, and transparency guide practice. Investigative journalism exposes corruption and holds power accountable. Press freedom remains threatened in many nations. Quality journalism remains essential for democratic societies.
