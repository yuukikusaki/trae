## Article 94: The Biology of Evolution

Evolution, the change in heritable traits of populations over generations, explains life's diversity and the adaptations organisms display. Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace independently proposed natural selection as evolution's mechanism. Individuals with advantageous traits survive and reproduce more successfully, increasing those traits' frequency.

Genetic variation provides the raw material for selection. Mutations introduce novel alleles. Sexual recombination shuffles existing variation into new combinations. Gene flow between populations transfers alleles across boundaries. Genetic drift randomly changes allele frequencies, especially in small populations.

Natural selection produces adaptations matching organisms to their environments. Camouflage enables predators to ambush prey and prey to avoid detection. Mimicry fools predators into avoiding harmless species resembling dangerous ones. Complex structures like eyes evolved gradually through intermediate stages providing incremental benefit.

Macroevolution encompasses speciation, extinction, and large-scale patterns across geological time. The fossil record documents transitions between major groups. Molecular phylogenetics reconstructs evolutionary relationships using DNA sequences. Evolutionary biology informs medicine, agriculture, and conservation while illuminating humanity's place in nature.

## Article 95: The Art of Poetry

Poetry distills language into concentrated forms that resonate emotionally and intellectually. Unlike prose, poetry arranges words according to rhythmic patterns, sonic effects, and visual spacing. Poets exploit meter, rhyme, assonance, and alliteration to create musical textures. Imagery evokes sensory experience through vivid language.

Poetic forms range from brief haiku capturing fleeting moments to epic narratives spanning generations. The sonnet, fourteen lines of iambic pentameter, explores themes of love, mortality, and art through volta shifts. The villanelle's repetitions reinforce obsession with persistent themes. Free verse abandons metrical conventions, trusting line breaks and white space to create structure.

Literary devices enable poetic effects. Metaphor compares directly without "like" or "as." Simile makes explicit comparisons. Personification attributes human qualities to abstract concepts or natural phenomena. Apostrophe addresses absent or abstract entities directly.

Poetry responds to historical contexts and shapes them. Protest poets amplify marginalized voices. Romantic poetry celebrated nature and individual emotion against industrialization. Imagists stripped language to essential elements. Contemporary poets experiment with hybrid forms, multimedia, and found texts. Poetry remains vital as a medium for compressed, resonant expression.

## Article 96: The Science of Aging

Aging, the accumulation of changes over time, affects all living organisms. Theories of aging divide between programmed and damage-based perspectives. Programmed theories suggest predetermined senescence regulated by genetic programs. Damage theories emphasize accumulated molecular injuries overwhelming repair mechanisms.

Cellular hallmarks of aging include genomic instability from DNA damage, telomere attrition limiting cellular divisions, epigenetic alterations affecting gene expression, and loss of proteostasis causing protein aggregation. Mitochondrial dysfunction generates reactive oxygen species that damage cellular components.

Cognitive abilities vary, with some functions remaining stable while processing speed declines. Sensory changes affect vision, hearing, taste, and smell.

Longevity research investigates interventions extending healthy lifespan. Caloric restriction extends lifespan in multiple species. Senolytics clear damaged senescent cells that accumulate with age. Rapamycin inhibits mTOR signaling linked to aging. Resveratrol activates sirtuins associated with metabolic health. Understanding aging may enable treatments extending human healthspan alongside lifespan.
