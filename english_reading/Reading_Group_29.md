## Article 85: The Biology of Reproduction

Reproduction ensures species survival across generations, occurring through sexual and asexual mechanisms. Sexual reproduction combines genetic material from two parents, generating offspring with novel allele combinations. Asexual reproduction produces genetically identical clones from a single parent, advantageous in stable environments.

Human reproduction begins with gamete formation through meiosis. Spermatogenesis occurs continuously in male testes, yielding millions of sperm daily. Oogenesis produces a single mature ovum per menstrual cycle, with billions of potential combinations through independent assortment and recombination. Fertilization fuses sperm and egg nuclei, restoring diploid chromosome number.

Pregnancy unfolds through trimesters of embryonic and fetal development. The blastocyst implants in the uterine wall, establishing the placenta that exchanges nutrients and wastes. Organ systems form during the embryonic period, followed by growth and maturation. Parturition, triggered by hormonal signals, involves uterine contractions that expel the fetus.

Reproductive technologies address infertility and genetic disorders. In vitro fertilization combines gametes in laboratory conditions. Preimplantation genetic testing screens embryos for chromosomal abnormalities. Contraception prevents pregnancy through barrier, hormonal, or intrauterine mechanisms. Understanding reproduction informs family planning, livestock breeding, and conservation efforts.

## Article 86: The Geography of Mountains

Mountains, elevated landforms rising prominently above surrounding terrain, cover approximately one-quarter of Earth's land surface. They form through tectonic collisions, volcanic activity, and erosion. Continental collisions buckle crust into ranges like the Himalayas, which rise from the meeting of Indian and Eurasian plates. Volcanic arcs, such as the Cascades and Andes, ring the Pacific.

Mountain ecosystems vary with altitude, creating distinct life zones from valley floors to summit scree. Lower slopes support forests; higher elevations give way to alpine meadows and eventually barren peaks. Mountains generate orographic precipitation as moist air rises, cools, and releases moisture on windward slopes, creating wet lowland environments.

Mountain peoples have developed distinctive cultures adapted to challenging terrain. Sherpas guide climbers on Everest. Quechua speakers inhabit Andean highlands. Tibetan nomads herd yak across vast plateaus. Traditional mountain agriculture terraces slopes, conserving soil and maximizing growing area.

Mountains provide essential ecosystem services, supplying freshwater to half of humanity. They harbor exceptional biodiversity and cultural heritage. Climate change disproportionately affects mountains, melting glaciers and shifting species ranges. Conservation efforts balance indigenous rights, sustainable development, and ecosystem protection.

## Article 87: The Science of Neurology

Neurology studies the nervous system, from cellular neuroscience to clinical management of neurological disorders. The brain, with approximately 86 billion neurons, processes information through electrical and chemical signaling. Synapses, junctions between neurons, transmit signals using neurotransmitters such as glutamate, GABA, and dopamine.

Brain regions serve specialized functions. The prefrontal cortex governs executive decisions and social behavior. The hippocampus forms declarative memories. The amygdala processes emotional significance. Motor cortex initiates voluntary movement. Sensory cortices interpret incoming signals from vision, hearing, and touch.

Neurological disorders result from diverse mechanisms. Stroke occurs when blood supply interrupts, killing neurons. Neurodegenerative diseases including Alzheimer's and Parkinson's involve progressive neuronal loss. Epilepsy produces uncontrolled electrical discharges causing seizures. Multiple sclerosis attacks myelin sheaths insulating axons.

Diagnostic neurology employs imaging, electrophysiology, and laboratory testing. CT and MRI reveal structural abnormalities. Electroencephalography records brain waves. Lumbar puncture analyzes cerebrospinal fluid. Treatments range from medication to neurosurgery. Emerging therapies include deep brain stimulation, stem cell transplantation, and gene therapy.
