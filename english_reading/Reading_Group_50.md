## Article 148: The Geography of Islands

Islands, landmasses surrounded by water, range from tiny reef outcrops to continental fragments. Oceanic islands rise from ocean floors through volcanic activity. Hawaiian islands exemplify hotspot volcanism creating chains across the Pacific. Continental islands separated from mainlands by rising seas or tectonic processes.

Island ecosystems exhibit distinctive characteristics. Endemic species, found nowhere else, evolve in isolation from mainland predators and competitors. Adaptive radiation produces extraordinary diversity, exemplified by Darwin's finches in the Galápagos. Limited space constrains populations and increases extinction vulnerability.

Human inhabitants developed island cultures shaped by maritime connections. Polynesian navigators colonized Pacific islands using celestial and wave navigation. Trade networks linked islands through exchange of goods, ideas, and genetic material. Colonial powers exploited islands for resources and strategic positions.

Contemporary island nations face unique challenges. Sea level rise threatens existence. Remote locations increase import dependence. Tourism provides income but creates environmental pressures. Exclusive economic zones grant rights to vast ocean resources.

## Article 149: The Science of Gastroenterology

Gastroenterology studies the digestive system, which processes food and absorbs nutrients. The GI tract extends from mouth to anus, including esophagus, stomach, small intestine, and large intestine. Accessory organs including liver, pancreas, and gallbladder aid digestion.

Gastroesophageal reflux disease causes heartburn when stomach acid backs into the esophagus. Peptic ulcers result from Helicobacter pylori infection or NSAID use. Inflammatory bowel disease includes Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, causing chronic intestinal inflammation.

Colon cancer arises from adenomatous polyps that progress over years. Screening colonoscopy enables early detection and removal. Irritable bowel syndrome involves abdominal pain with altered bowel habits without structural abnormalities. Celiac disease results from immune reaction to gluten, damaging intestinal villi.

Endoscopic procedures visualize and treat GI conditions. Colonoscopy examines the colon; upper endoscopy evaluates esophagus, stomach, and duodenum. Capsule endoscopy swallows a camera that photographs the small intestine. ERCP accesses bile ducts through duodenum.

## Article 150: The Science of Oncology

Oncology studies cancer, a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and spread. Carcinogenesis involves accumulated genetic damage from environmental exposures, inherited mutations, and random errors. Oncogenes accelerate growth; tumor suppressor genes normally restrain proliferation.

Cancers are classified by tissue of origin. Carcinomas arise from epithelial cells and comprise 80-90 percent of cancers. Sarcomas develop in connective tissues. Leukemias affect blood-forming cells; lymphomas arise in lymphatic tissues. Each type requires distinct treatment approaches.

Surgery removes solid tumors when localized. Radiation kills tumor cells with focused beams. Chemotherapy uses cytotoxic drugs affecting rapidly dividing cells. Targeted therapies inhibit specific molecular pathways. Immunotherapy activates immune responses against tumors. Combination approaches improve outcomes.

Screening enables early detection before symptoms develop. Mammography, colonoscopy, Pap smears, and low-dose CT screening reduce cancer mortality. Treatment decisions consider tumor stage, molecular characteristics, and patient preferences. Survivorship care addresses long-term effects.
