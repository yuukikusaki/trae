## Article 127: The Geography of Tundra

Tundra, treeless regions occurring in Arctic, Antarctic, and alpine zones, feature harsh conditions limiting plant growth. Short growing seasons, extreme cold, and permafrost prevent tree establishment. Vegetation consists of grasses, sedges, mosses, and lichens.

Arctic tundra extends across northern latitudes in Russia, Canada, and Alaska. Antarctic tundra occurs on sub-Antarctic islands and the southernmost continents. Alpine tundra exists above treeline on mountains worldwide.

Extremely cold temperatures characterize tundra climates. Mean temperatures rarely exceed ten degrees Celsius even in summer. Permafrost, permanently frozen ground, underlies most tundra, preventing deep root penetration. Summer thaw creates waterlogged surface soils.

Tundra ecosystems support specialized wildlife. Caribou and reindeer migrate across Arctic tundra in vast herds. Musk oxen, arctic foxes, and snowy owls exemplify cold adaptations. Insects emerge briefly during summer to feed and reproduce.

Climate change disproportionately affects tundra regions. Warming accelerates permafrost thaw, releasing stored methane. Ice-dependent species face habitat loss. Industrial development including oil extraction creates additional pressures.

## Article 128: The Science of Nephrology

Nephrology studies kidney function and disease. The kidneys filter blood, remove waste, regulate fluid and electrolyte balance, and produce hormones controlling blood pressure and red blood cell production. Each kidney contains approximately one million nephrons, functional filtering units.

Chronic kidney disease progresses through stages as nephrons progressively lose function. Diabetes and hypertension cause most cases. Early stages may lack symptoms; advanced disease requires dialysis or transplantation. End-stage renal disease necessitates renal replacement therapy.

Dialysis substitutes for failing kidneys. Hemodialysis circulates blood through machines containing semipermeable membranes. Peritoneal dialysis uses the abdominal lining as a natural filter. Both require dietary restrictions and frequent treatment sessions.

Kidney transplantation offers superior outcomes when feasible. Live donor kidneys function longer than deceased donor organs. Immunosuppressive medications prevent rejection. Organ shortage drives innovation in xenotransplantation and bioartificial kidneys.

## Article 129: The History of Science

Science, the systematic pursuit of natural knowledge, emerged from philosophy and natural history. Ancient Greeks made foundational contributions, including geometric proofs and astronomical observations. Islamic scholars preserved and extended classical knowledge during Europe's medieval period.

The Scientific Revolution of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries transformed understanding. Copernicus proposed heliocentric astronomy. Galileo championed observation and experimentation. Newton synthesized celestial and terrestrial mechanics with universal gravitation.

Laboratory practice formalized with Lavoisier's chemistry and Volta's electricity. Professional disciplines emerged in the nineteenth century. Darwin's theory of evolution and Maxwell's electromagnetism represented major advances.

Twentieth-century science saw extraordinary progress. Relativity and quantum mechanics revolutionized physics. Molecular biology deciphered genetic mechanisms. Computing enabled simulation and data analysis at unprecedented scales. Science continues advancing, revealing deeper understanding of nature and ourselves.
