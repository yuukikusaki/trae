## Article 166: The Science of Parasitology

Parasitology studies organisms living on or within hosts, deriving nourishment at host expense. Parasites include protozoa, worms, insects, and fungi causing human, animal, and plant diseases. Host-parasite relationships drive coevolution.

Malaria, caused by Plasmodium protozoa transmitted by mosquitoes, remains one of humanity's greatest infectious disease burdens. Symptoms include fever, chills, and anemia. Prevention focuses on mosquito control and prophylactic medications.

Intestinal parasites include tapeworms, hookworms, and roundworms affecting billions globally. These parasites cause malnutrition, anemia, and developmental delays. Sanitation improvements and deworming programs reduce transmission.

Ectoparasites including lice, fleas, and ticks feed externally and transmit pathogens. Bedbugs cause itchy infestations without disease transmission. Managing parasites requires understanding life cycles,宿主 specificity, and transmission routes.

## Article 167: The History of Exploration

Human exploration has expanded geographic knowledge and reshaped civilizations. Early humans migrated from Africa to populate every continent except Antarctica. Ocean crossings enabled colonization of Australia and remote Pacific islands.

European Age of Exploration beginning in the fifteenth century connected previously isolated regions. Portuguese sailors rounded Africa to reach Asia. Columbus encountered the Americas; subsequent voyages mapped coastlines and revealed planetary scale. Magellan's expedition proved Earth is spherical.

Polar exploration tested human endurance in extreme conditions. Amundsen reached the South Pole in 1911; Scott's tragic expedition followed. Peary claimed the North Pole. These achievements demonstrated human capability and opened previously inaccessible regions.

Space exploration extends exploration beyond Earth. Satellites enable communications and observation. Astronauts visited the Moon; robotic probes have visited every planet. Mars rovers search for signs of life. The cosmos awaits future explorers.

## Article 168: The Science of Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics studies energy, heat, and their transformations. The first law, energy conservation, states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. Systems possess internal energy that changes through heat transfer and work.

The second law introduces entropy, quantifying disorder and process direction. Heat spontaneously flows from hot to cold objects. Entropy increases in isolated systems, approaching maximum equilibrium. Perpetual motion machines violate thermodynamic principles.

Heat engines convert heat into work, powering transportation and electricity generation. Carnot efficiency sets theoretical maximum efficiency based on temperature differences. Refrigerators move heat from cold to hot reservoirs.

Statistical mechanics connects microscopic particle behavior to macroscopic thermodynamics. Temperature reflects average kinetic energy; entropy measures microstate availability. Third law establishes absolute zero entropy for perfect crystals.
