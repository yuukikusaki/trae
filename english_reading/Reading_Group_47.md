## Article 139: The Science of Marine Biology

Marine biology studies organisms inhabiting oceans, from microscopic plankton to massive whales. The ocean covers 71 percent of Earth's surface and contains trenches exceeding 10,000 meters depth. Marine life has evolved extraordinary diversity in response to oceanic conditions.

Plankton, drifting organisms, form the base of marine food webs. Phytoplankton, photosynthetic single-celled algae, produce approximately half of global oxygen. Zooplankton consume phytoplankton and are consumed by larger animals. Nekton, active swimmers, include fish, squid, and marine mammals.

Coral reefs, constructed by colonial cnidarians, support approximately 25 percent of marine species despite covering less than one percent of ocean floor. They require warm, clear, shallow waters and are extremely sensitive to temperature and acidity changes.

Marine mammals including whales, dolphins, and seals evolved from terrestrial ancestors, returning to ocean environments. Their adaptations include streamlined bodies, blubber insulation, and breath-holding capacity. Protecting marine ecosystems requires addressing overfishing, pollution, and climate change.

## Article 140: The Science of Cardiology

Cardiology studies the heart and circulatory system. The heart, a muscular pump approximately the size of a fist, beats 100,000 times daily, circulating 5 liters per minute at rest. Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle itself with blood.

Coronary artery disease, the leading cause of death globally, results from atherosclerotic plaque accumulating in vessel walls. Plaque rupture triggers clot formation, blocking blood flow and causing heart attacks. Risk factors include smoking, high cholesterol, hypertension, diabetes, and family history.

Arrhythmias, abnormal heart rhythms, range from benign extrasystoles to life-threatening ventricular fibrillation. Atrial fibrillation increases stroke risk through blood clot formation. Pacemakers correct slow rhythms; implantable defibrillators terminate dangerous fast rhythms.

Heart failure occurs when the heart cannot pump adequately to meet body demands. Symptoms include shortness of breath, fluid retention, and fatigue. Treatment includes medications, devices, and ultimately heart transplantation. Advances in prevention, diagnosis, and treatment continue improving cardiovascular outcomes.

## Article 141: The History of Exploration

Human exploration of Earth and beyond has expanded knowledge and reshaped civilizations. Early humans migrated from Africa to populate every continent except Antarctica. Ocean crossings enabled colonization of Australia and remote Pacific islands.

European Age of Exploration beginning in the fifteenth century connected previously isolated world regions. Portuguese sailors rounded Africa to reach Asia. Columbus encountered the Americas; subsequent voyages mapped coastlines and revealed planetary scale. Magellan circumnavigated the globe, proving Earth's sphericity.

Polar exploration tested human endurance in extreme conditions. Amundsen reached the South Pole in 1911; Scott's tragic expedition followed months later. Peary claimed the North Pole; disputed measurements continue.

Space exploration extends exploration beyond Earth. Satellites enable global communications and observation. Astronauts visited the Moon; robotic probes have visited every planet. Mars rovers search for signs of past or present life. The cosmos awaits future explorers.
