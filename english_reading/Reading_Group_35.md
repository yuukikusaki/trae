## Article 103: The Science of Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics studies energy, heat, and their transformations. The first law, energy conservation, states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. Systems possess internal energy that changes through heat transfer and work done. Heat, energy in transit due to temperature difference, differs from work, energy transfer through macroscopic force.

The second law introduces entropy, quantifying disorder and the direction of natural processes. Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold objects, never reverse. Perpetual motion machines violate either the first or second law. Entropy increases in isolated systems, approaching maximum equilibrium.

Heat engines convert heat into work, powering transportation and electricity generation. Carnot efficiency sets theoretical maximum efficiency based on temperature differences. Refrigerators move heat from cold to hot reservoirs, requiring work input. Entropy considerations limit achievable efficiencies.

Statistical mechanics connects microscopic particle behavior to macroscopic thermodynamics. Temperature reflects average kinetic energy. Entropy measures the number of microstates consistent with macroscopic conditions. Third law establishes absolute zero entropy for perfect crystals. Thermodynamics underlies engines, refrigerators, power plants, and understanding of physical, chemical, and biological systems.

## Article 104: The History of Photography

Photography transformed visual culture, enabling unprecedented documentation and artistic expression. Niepce's heliography required eight-hour exposures; Daguerre's refined process reduced times dramatically. George Eastman democratized photography with the Kodak camera, providing film processing services. Color photography emerged through Autochrome and Kodachrome processes.

Digital photography replaced film with electronic sensors. CMOS and CCD sensors convert photons into electrical signals. ISO sensitivity, aperture, and shutter speed control exposure. RAW files preserve maximum data for post-processing. Smartphone integration placed cameras in billions of pockets.

Photography serves journalism, science, art, and personal memory. Photojournalists document history; scientific imaging ranges from microscopy to astrophysics. Fine art photographers create works exhibited in galleries worldwide. Computational photography enables HDR imaging, portrait mode, and night sight capabilities exceeding traditional optics.

## Article 105: The Biology of Genetics

Genetics studies heredity and variation, explaining how traits pass between generations. Mendel's experiments with pea plants revealed particulate inheritance principles. Alleles, alternative gene versions, segregate during gamete formation and combine randomly during fertilization.

DNA, deoxyribonucleic acid, encodes genetic information through nucleotide sequences. Double helix structure permits faithful replication. Genes, functional units, specify proteins through transcription and translation. Gene expression is regulated by promoters, enhancers, and epigenetic modifications.

Human genetics involves approximately 20,000 protein-coding genes distributed across 23 chromosome pairs. Variations range from single nucleotide changes to large deletions and duplications. Dominant alleles manifest in heterozygotes; recessive alleles require homozygosity.

Genetic technologies enable diagnosis, treatment, and modification. Polymerase chain reaction amplifies tiny DNA quantities. DNA sequencing determines nucleotide order. CRISPR editing enables precise modifications. Gene therapy treats inherited disorders. Population genetics studies allele frequency changes. Genetic knowledge transforms medicine and biology.
