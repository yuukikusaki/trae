## Article 130: The Science of Dermatology

Dermatology studies skin, the body's largest organ, serving protective, sensory, and regulatory functions. Skin comprises the epidermis, dermis, and subcutaneous layers. Specialized cells include melanocytes producing pigment and Langerhans cells mediating immunity.

Common skin conditions include acne, eczema, psoriasis, and skin cancer. Acne involves pilosebaceous unit inflammation; treatments include topical retinoids and antibiotics. Eczema, or atopic dermatitis, features itchy, inflamed skin responsive to moisturizers and anti-inflammatories. Psoriasis produces scaly plaques from rapid epidermal turnover.

Skin cancer includes basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma. Ultraviolet radiation exposure drives most cases. Early detection improves outcomes; melanoma requires prompt excision. Mohs surgery precisely removes skin cancers while sparing normal tissue.

## Article 131: The Geography of Savannas

Savannas, grasslands with scattered trees, occupy approximately twenty percent of Earth's land surface. They occur in tropical and subtropical regions with seasonal rainfall. Dry seasons favor grasses; scattered trees tolerate fire and drought.

African savannas support iconic wildlife including elephants, giraffes, lions, and zebras. Migration routes follow seasonal rainfall patterns. Herbivore populations shape vegetation through grazing and browsing. Predators regulate prey populations.

South American llanos and campos feature similar grass-tree mosaics. Australian savannas include eucalyptus trees and marsupial grazers. Asian savannas occur in India and Southeast Asia.

Fire plays crucial roles in savanna ecology. Dry season burning maintains grass dominance against tree encroachment. Lightning-sparked and human-ignited fires recycle nutrients and create habitat heterogeneity. Suppression policies alter fire regimes and vegetation dynamics.

Human activities convert savannas for agriculture and ranching. Cattle grazing represents major land use. Conservation areas protect remaining wilderness; managed burns maintain ecological processes.

## Article 132: The Science of Pathology

Pathology studies disease mechanisms at cellular and tissue levels. Anatomical pathology examines structural changes in organs and tissues. Clinical pathology analyzes body fluids including blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid. Molecular pathology investigates genetic and biochemical alterations.

Inflammation represents the body's response to injury, manifesting as redness, heat, swelling, and pain. Acute inflammation resolves quickly; chronic inflammation persists and causes tissue damage. Granulomatous inflammation features specialized immune cells surrounding persistent irritants.

Neoplasia, abnormal cell growth, may be benign or malignant. Benign tumors remain localized; malignant tumors invade and metastasize. Carcinomas arise from epithelial cells; sarcomas from mesenchymal cells. Grading and staging guide treatment decisions.

Autopsy, examination of deceased patients, reveals disease processes and identifies diagnostic errors. Forensics applies pathology to legal investigations, determining cause and manner of death. Pathological findings inform clinical practice and advance medical knowledge.
