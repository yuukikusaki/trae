## Article 118: The Science of Infectious Disease

Infectious diseases, caused by pathogens including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites, have shaped human history. Epidemics and pandemics have killed millions, toppling empires and transforming societies. Understanding transmission, pathogenesis, and control enables prevention and treatment.

Pathogens spread through various routes. Respiratory infections transmit via droplets and aerosols. Foodborne pathogens contaminate water and edibles. Vector-borne diseases involve arthropods such as mosquitoes and ticks. Sexual transmission requires intimate contact.

Host defenses include physical barriers, innate immune responses, and adaptive immunity. Vaccines stimulate protective immunity without disease. Antimicrobials kill or inhibit pathogens. Antibiotics target bacteria specifically; antivirals inhibit viral replication.

Emerging infectious diseases pose ongoing threats. Deforestation exposes humans to novel pathogens. Climate change shifts vector distributions. Global travel enables rapid international spread. Pandemic preparedness requires surveillance, rapid diagnostics, and flexible vaccine platforms.

## Article 119: The Geography of Rainforests

Tropical rainforests occur in equatorial regions receiving over 2000 millimeters of annual rainfall. The Amazon, Congo, and Southeast Asian forests represent the three major blocks. These ecosystems harbor extraordinary biodiversity, hosting perhaps half of Earth's species despite covering only seven percent of land.

Forest structure includes distinct layers. The emergent layer contains towering trees above the continuous canopy. The canopy, dense and leafy, captures most sunlight. Understory saplings and ground vegetation tolerate low light levels. Buttresses and epiphytes characterize tropical trees.

Rainforest soils are surprisingly nutrient-poor. Most nutrients cycle through living biomass rather than accumulating in soil. Rapid decomposition recycles fallen leaves and dead organisms. Nutrient scarcity limits agricultural potential.

Deforestation releases stored carbon and destroys habitat. Cattle ranching, soy cultivation, palm oil production, and logging drive forest loss. REDD+ programs pay developing nations to preserve forests. Sustainable forestry and agroforestry offer alternatives to complete conversion.

## Article 120: The Science of Endocrinology

Endocrinology studies hormones, chemical messengers secreted by glands into the bloodstream. Hormones regulate metabolism, growth, reproduction, stress responses, and behavior. The endocrine system coordinates body functions through feedback loops maintaining homeostasis.

The hypothalamus-pituitary axis controls many endocrine functions. The hypothalamus produces releasing hormones that stimulate the pituitary gland. Pituitary hormones then stimulate peripheral glands including thyroid, adrenal, and gonads. Target organs respond with hormone secretion and physiological effects.

Thyroid disorders exemplify endocrine pathology. Hypothyroidism slows metabolism, causing fatigue and weight gain. Hyperthyroidism accelerates metabolism, producing anxiety and weight loss. Goiter, enlargement of the thyroid gland, may indicate iodine deficiency or autoimmune disease.

Diabetes mellitus involves insulin deficiency or resistance. Type 1 diabetes results from autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing beta cells. Type 2 diabetes develops through insulin resistance and relative deficiency. Management includes diet, exercise, medications, and insulin replacement.
