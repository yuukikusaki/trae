## Article 163: The Science of Neurology

Neurology studies the nervous system, including brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves. Neurons communicate through electrical impulses and chemical neurotransmitters. The brain's 86 billion neurons form networks enabling perception, cognition, and behavior.

Neurological disorders result from diverse mechanisms. Stroke interrupts blood supply, killing neurons. Neurodegenerative diseases involve progressive neuronal loss. Epilepsy generates uncontrolled electrical discharges causing seizures. Multiple sclerosis destroys myelin.

Diagnostic neurology employs imaging, electrophysiology, and laboratory testing. CT and MRI reveal structural abnormalities. Electroencephalography records brain waves. Lumbar puncture analyzes cerebrospinal fluid.

Treatment ranges from medication to neurosurgery. Thrombolysis and thrombectomy restore blood flow in acute stroke. Anticonvulsants control seizure disorders. Deep brain stimulation helps movement disorders. Emerging gene and cell therapies offer hope for previously untreatable conditions.

## Article 164: The History of Agriculture

Agriculture, cultivating plants and animals for food and materials, began approximately 10,000 years ago during the Neolithic Revolution. Domestication of wild grasses transformed hunter-gatherers into farmers. Villages replaced camps; populations expanded with reliable food supplies.

Ancient civilizations developed irrigation, terraces, and crop rotation. Mesopotamian canals enabled irrigation. Egyptian agriculture depended on Nile flooding. Chinese farmers terraced hillsides. Agricultural surplus supported urbanization and specialization.

The British Agricultural Revolution introduced crop rotation, selective breeding, and mechanization. Enclosure consolidated fields for efficient farming. Tractors and combines mechanized field work. The Green Revolution developed high-yield varieties feeding billions.

Contemporary agriculture employs GPS guidance, genetic engineering, and precision irrigation. Challenges include environmental degradation, resource depletion, and food security. Sustainable practices aim to maintain productivity while reducing negative impacts.

## Article 165: The Geography of Coastal Zones

Coastal zones, where land meets sea, support diverse ecosystems and dense human populations. Approximately forty percent of the global population lives within 100 kilometers of coasts. These areas provide critical habitat, food resources, and economic opportunities.

Shorelines feature various landforms shaped by waves, tides, and geology. Rocky cliffs resist erosion; sandy beaches accumulate sediment. Deltas form where rivers deposit sediments. Coral reefs support exceptional biodiversity.

Coastal ecosystems include salt marshes, mangroves, estuaries, and beaches. Salt marshes shelter juvenile fish and filter pollutants. Mangrove forests stabilize shorelines and protect against storms. Estuaries provide nursery habitat for commercial fisheries.

Sea level rise threatens coastal communities. Global warming raises ocean levels; erosion accelerates. Storm surge flooding increases. Managed retreat, hard protection, and nature-based solutions offer adaptation strategies.
